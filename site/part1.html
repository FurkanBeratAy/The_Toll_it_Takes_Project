Schools near monitored corridors

NYC Admin Code §24-163 limits vehicle idling to 1 minute within a school zone (vs. 3 minutes citywide), enforceable by any citizen who films a violation through DEP's Idling Complaint System (Local Law 58, 2018). This law applies uniquely to schools — a policy lever the corridor-level analysis cannot surface.

Did EJ-designated sites fare worse?

EJ status is confounded with zone status in this data: every in-zone monitor except Queensboro sits in a designated tract, and so does every outside-zone monitor with a full pre-toll record. Panel estimates (site + date fixed effects, 11 sites) of an EJ gap run from −0.38 to +0.02 µg/m³ depending on flag definition, none significant (p = 0.65–0.96). What the data shows is geography: inside the zone improved; the two South Bronx corridors did not.

Robustness checks

Five independent tests of the traffic-diversion hypothesis. The main ITS estimates are underpowered (Mott Haven p = 0.61; five ITS sites, eleven in the panel) and PM2.5 is driven largely by regional sources. The checks below probe whether the null results are informative or whether a signal emerges under sharper designs. Hunts Point is excluded from all checks (monitor offline Sept 2023–March 2025, no valid step-change at the toll date).

Hunts Point is omitted: its monitor was offline Sept 2023 to March 2025, so no pre/post comparison at the toll date is possible.

1. Weather-adjusted estimates

Daily wind speed, temperature, precipitation, relative humidity, and dominant wind-direction sector (8 compass bins) added to the full ITS model. Compares 95% CI width to the original specification.

Original vs. weather-adjusted β_post with 95% CI

Adding five daily weather covariates (temperature, wind speed, relative humidity, precipitation, wind direction) tightened the 95% CI at 8 of 11 sites. No point estimate changed sign, indicating the baseline results are not artefacts of uncontrolled meteorology.

2. Peak-vs-overnight PM2.5 difference

The toll is 75% cheaper overnight. If diversion is toll-driven, peak-hour PM2.5 should rise relative to overnight. Daily outcome: mean peak PM2.5 minus mean overnight PM2.5 (≥8 peak hours, ≥5 overnight hours required). QC and VW differences used as controls.

ITS β_post on peak-minus-overnight PM2.5 (all days and weekdays only)

At Mott Haven, peak-hour PM2.5 was 0.219 µg/m³ higher than overnight after the toll (p=0.419). The weekday-only version gives β=+0.058 (p=0.851). Cross Bronx Expy is the one site where this test is informative: all-days β=+0.60 (p=0.089), weekday-only β=+0.90 (p=0.039) — the only significant result in 22 peak/overnight tests across 11 sites. Regional weather differences cancel in the differenced series, so this result is harder to attribute to non-traffic sources. One significant result in 22 tests is consistent with chance, but it is the Bronx EJ site closest to the Throgs Neck diversion route and therefore the most plausible candidate for a real effect. BQE and SI Expwy, each with only ~4 months of pre-toll data in a single season, should be read with caution.

3. Placebo-in-time

Using pre-toll data only, a fake intervention is placed on the 1st of each month from July 2021 through June 2024 (up to 36 placebos). The long-baseline QC-only spec is used for all sites. Each placebo requires ≥90 observed days on each side and ≥60% data coverage in the surrounding 6-month windows. Empirical p = share of |β_placebo| ≥ |β_observed|.

Distribution of placebo β_post by site (red line = observed long-baseline β)

The placebo test is inconclusive for this dataset. The long-baseline QC-only specification is highly unstable: placebo β distributions have SD of 7–17 µg/m³ across the five sites (Queensboro placebo mean +15.7 µg/m³; Williamsburg has only 3 valid placebos), driven by large data gaps (268–791 days), the COVID period, and wildfire months. Observed effects of 1–3 µg/m³ are not exceptional against a baseline that swings ±15 µg/m³. The placebo test cannot distinguish signal from instability, so empirical p-values are not reported. The long-baseline column in the ITS table should be read as a sensitivity note, not a primary result.

4. Pooled panel (site + date fixed effects)

All monitoring sites with ≥30 pre-toll days in the VW window are stacked into a balanced panel. Site and date fixed effects absorb site-specific baselines and shared daily shocks (date FE replaces the QC covariate). Two specifications: (A) post × site — per-site toll effects; (B) post × EJ + post × in-CRZ — differential effect by environmental-justice status. Wild cluster bootstrap with Webb weights (B=9,999, G clusters). Hunts Point excluded (zero pre-toll observations in the VW window).

Spec A: per-site toll effect (site + date FE, cluster-robust CI)

Panel estimates of an EJ gap range from −0.38 to +0.02 µg/m³ depending on how the flag is defined (B1: −0.07, p=0.77; B2: −0.38, p=0.65; B3: +0.02, p=0.96), none significant. EJ status is confounded with zone status in this data: every in-zone monitor except Queensboro sits in a designated tract, and so does every outside-zone monitor with a full pre-toll record. The panel cannot separate the zone effect from the EJ effect with the current monitoring network.

5. Mechanism check

In the pre-toll period (2022–2024), Mott Haven daily PM2.5 is regressed on Throgs Neck Bridge daily crossings (per 1,000 vehicles), Queens College, seasonal terms, day-of-week dummies, and weather covariates (HAC SE, maxlags=30). The estimated slope is multiplied by the post-toll Throgs Neck residual (+2,041 veh/day) to produce an implied PM2.5 change, then compared to the observed ITS step-change.

Mott Haven PM2.5 vs Throgs Neck volume (pre-toll, regression line shown)

In the pre-toll period (2022–2024), each additional 1,000 vehicles/day at Throgs Neck Bridge is associated with β=+0.0181 µg/m³ at Mott Haven (p=0.272, HAC SE, n=144). Multiplying by the post-toll Throgs Neck residual of +2041 veh/day implies +0.04 µg/m³ — about one-eighth of the observed ITS step-change of +0.30 µg/m³, so traffic diversion via Throgs Neck cannot account for most of the Mott Haven increase. However, consistency of magnitudes alone does not establish causation; unmeasured confounders could produce the same pattern.

6. Bronx NO2 (EPA AQS hourly, 2022–2025)

Hourly NO2 from two EPA AQS SLAMS monitors in the Bronx — IS 52 (681 Kelly St, South Bronx; Site 110) and Pfizer Lab Site (Southern Blvd & 200th St, North Bronx; Site 133) — aggregated to daily means and compared before and after the toll. Background control: daily mean of Queens EPA sites 124 and 125. Longer baseline ITS uses 2022-01-01 start with HAC SE (maxlags=30). Note: neither monitor is on the Bruckner Expressway or the Major Deegan corridor.

Raw 2024-vs-2025 calendar-year comparison: IS 52 fell −0.43 ppb (from 14.70 to 14.27); Pfizer Lab fell −0.38 ppb (from 12.48 to 12.10). The Queens control sites changed +0.21 ppb (Site 124) and −0.68 ppb (Site 125) over the same period — changes of comparable magnitude and direction. The longer-baseline ITS (2022-01-01 to present) gives β = −5.99 ppb at IS 52 (p = 0.079, n_pre = 1,089) and β = −3.68 ppb at Pfizer Lab (p = 0.240, n_pre = 1,097), neither significant at α = 0.05. Placebo tests (pre-toll data only, 24 placebo dates July 2022 – June 2024) give empirical p = 0.29 for IS 52 and 0.50 for Pfizer Lab, meaning betas this large arise routinely from seasonal variation in the pre-toll record. No toll effect on Bronx NO2 is detectable.

8. Step-only ITS — post×t term removed
